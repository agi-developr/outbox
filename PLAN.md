# What I think you want, and what I need from you

## What you actually said across this session

You said the mailbox should be AI-native. Then you said a cheap model should read every message
and classify it. Then you said event invites should show what you gain and give you two buttons.
Then you said you pitched two CEOs and you are forgetful, so an agent should own the follow-up.
Then you said a normal CRM is too bulky. Then you said this feels like a real consumer need.
Then you said an AI should phone you, ask you a lot of generated questions, and build from your answers.

The through-line is not email. It is this: **you have obligations living in your head and in
scattered threads, and you want an agent to hold them for you.** Email is just where most of them
arrive. The mailbox I built made email the subject. It should have been the input.

## The product, in one sentence

An agent that holds every open loop you have with another human, chases the ones that need chasing,
and asks you only the questions it genuinely cannot answer alone.

## The three parts

**1. It takes things in.** Email first, because email has an API and no permission problem.
Later: texts, calls, DMs. Each incoming thing becomes an open loop or gets closed automatically.

**2. It holds the loops.** Not folders, not a pipeline, not stages you drag. One list of
"things that are open with a human", each with who, what you want from them, and when it next moves.
This is the CRM replacement. It is thin because you never fill it in — it fills itself.

**3. It interviews you.** This is your newest idea and I think it is the strongest one.
When the agent hits something it cannot decide, it does not sit silent and it does not guess.
It asks. By voice, by call, in a batch, whenever you are free. Ten questions in three minutes,
answered out loud, and every open loop moves. That is the interface. Not a board, not an inbox.

## Why the interview part is the real idea

Every AI product today fails at the same point: it does not know enough about you to act, and the
work of telling it is more expensive than doing the task yourself. Typing context is the tax.
Talking is not. You said it yourself tonight — you do not know what you want, but you would know
if something asked you the right question.

That means the product is not "AI reads your email". It is **"AI extracts your intent, then acts
on your behalf, and the extraction happens by voice because typing is the bottleneck."**
Email is the first place it proves useful. It is not the boundary.

## What I would build tonight, if there were time

One screen. A list of open loops. A phone icon in the corner. You tap it, the agent calls you,
asks you the six questions blocking the six loops, you answer out loud, and the list updates live
in front of the room while you talk. That is the demo. Nobody has shown that on a stage.

Answer any of these out loud or in one word each. Skip anything you do not care about.

1. Tonight's demo: the loop list, or the voice interview? Only one fits the clock.
2. Is the person using this you specifically, or a normal person with a job?

4. Voice: does it call your phone, or do you talk to it in the browser?
5. When it asks you questions, is it one at a time all day, or a batch you sit down for?
6. Does it handle inbound only, or does it also start conversations you never opened?
7. Is the follow-up chasing the headline feature, or a side effect?
8. If this replaces something you pay for today, what is it replacing?
9. What is the moment where you would say "that was worth money"?
10. Name one thing you are currently forgetting that this should have caught.
